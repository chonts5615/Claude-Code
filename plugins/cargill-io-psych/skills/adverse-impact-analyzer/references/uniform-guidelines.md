# Uniform Guidelines — adverse impact in plain language

Source: *Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures*, 29 CFR Part 1607
(1978), jointly adopted by the EEOC, DOL/OFCCP, DOJ, and the Civil Service
Commission. **Re-verify verbatim text** at law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/part-1607
or ecfr.gov before quoting regulation in a deliverable.

## §4D — the four-fifths (80%) rule

A selection rate for any race, sex, or ethnic group that is **less than
four-fifths (4/5, or 80%)** of the rate for the group with the **highest**
selection rate is generally regarded by the enforcement agencies as **evidence**
of adverse impact.

Key qualifiers (do not drop them):
- It is explicitly a **"rule of thumb,"** not a legal definition of
  discrimination, and **not a safe harbor**. A ratio ≥ 0.80 does not prove
  fairness.
- **Smaller differences** can still be adverse impact where they are
  "significant in both statistical and practical terms," or where the user's
  actions discouraged applicants.
- **Larger differences** may *not* indicate adverse impact where the numbers are
  too small to be reliable, or where special recruiting produced an atypical
  applicant pool.

**Computation:** selection rate = persons selected ÷ applicants, computed **per
group, per job**. Impact ratio = focal-group rate ÷ highest-group rate. Flag
< 0.80.

## §4C — the "bottom line"

Adverse impact is generally assessed on the **overall** selection process first.
If the total process shows no adverse impact, the agencies generally will not
require validity evidence for individual components (with exceptions, e.g.
§15A(2)(b)). When the bottom line *does* show impact, component-level analysis
follows.

## §5 / §14 — the three validation strategies

If a procedure causes adverse impact and is still used, it must be supported by
validity evidence under one of three strategies:

| Strategy | Shows | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Criterion-related** | Scores predict job performance (statistical link) | Larger samples, measurable performance criteria |
| **Content** | The procedure is a representative sample of the work/KSAOs | Work samples, job-knowledge tests; rests on a job analysis |
| **Construct** | The procedure measures a job-relevant construct | Abilities/traits with established theory and measurement |

§14 gives the technical standards for each. Name the strategy you would rely on;
do not leave it implicit.

## §15 — documentation

Maintain, by job: (a) persons hired/promoted/terminated by sex and (where
appropriate) race/national origin; (b) applicants for hire/promotion by sex and
race/national origin; (c) the selection procedures used. The deliverable should
reference these categories so the analysis is reproducible and audit-ready.

## Deliverable structure

**Excel — Impact-Ratio Matrix:** one row per group with applicants, selected,
selection rate, impact ratio vs highest, 4/5ths flag, significance p,
small-sample warning; a `Summary` tab (process, basis, period, overall read).
Highlight < 0.80 (amber) and significant shortfalls (red).

**Word — Defensibility Narrative:**
1. Process, job, period, basis.
2. Method (4/5ths + significance test; data source; §15 record basis).
3. Results table and the highest-rate reference group.
4. The rule-of-thumb framing and the practical/statistical/ small-sample caveats.
5. If impact is present: the validation strategy that would support the procedure
   and any remediation (e.g. method change via `selection-method-advisor`).
6. Disclaimer (statistics, not a legal conclusion).
