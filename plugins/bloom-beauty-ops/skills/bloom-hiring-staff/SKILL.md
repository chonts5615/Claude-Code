# Bloom Hiring & Staff

Bring on lash techs and suite renters the right way: a clear job post, a short
**structured** interview, a **behaviorally-anchored trial-day scorecard**, a clean
onboarding checklist, and a renter-vetting rubric. Real selection rigor, sized for
a salon — fair to candidates and defensible for the owner.

## Why This Skill Exists

A lash studio's quality *is* the artist; a suite business's income *is* the
renters who pay and stay. Hiring on gut feel is how a great-on-paper tech turns
into redo's and refunds, or a charming applicant turns into a chronic
late-payer. The fix is the same discipline used for executive selection — ask
every candidate the **same job-relevant questions**, score a **real work sample**
(a trial set) against **observable anchors**, and decide on evidence — just
scaled down so it takes an afternoon, not a quarter. This skill brings that
discipline to Bloom, and surfaces the two things small owners most often get
wrong: worker classification and licensing.

## Process

### Step 1 — Define the role on observable terms
List the must-have outcomes (e.g. "produces a clean, retentioned classic full
set in ≤2h," "shows valid MN licensure," "communicates with clients warmly").
Keep it to what you can observe in a trial and a conversation.

### Step 2 — Write the job post or renter listing
From `references/hiring-process.md` templates, in Bloom's voice: the role, what
makes Bloom a great place to work/rent, must-haves (incl. **valid Minnesota
license** for lash services), pay/rent model, and how to apply. Honest and warm,
no inflated promises.

### Step 3 — Structured screen + interview (same for everyone)
Use the short structured question set in `references/hiring-process.md` — the
same job-relevant questions, same order, for every candidate, each with a simple
anchored rating. Standardization is what makes it fair and predictive.

### Step 4 — Trial day (the work sample — your best predictor)
Run a paid trial set scored on the **behaviorally-anchored trial scorecard**
(craft, sanitation, retention technique, client interaction, professionalism).
A real work sample is among the most valid, least-biased signals you can get.
See `references/hiring-process.md` for the BARS anchors.

### Step 5 — Classification & licensing check (do not skip)
Before any offer or lease, work through `references/classification.md`: the
independent-contractor-vs-employee factors (IRS + Minnesota), what a suite-renter
relationship requires to genuinely be a renter and not an employee, MN
licensing/sanitation, and the documents involved. **Flag, don't decide** —
recommend confirming with a qualified professional.

### Step 6 — Onboarding / move-in
Produce the onboarding checklist (employee/contractor) or the renter move-in
checklist and a renter-vetting summary from `references/hiring-process.md`.

## Standard Disclaimer

> Hiring and leasing support — **not legal, tax, or HR-compliance advice**.
> Worker classification (independent contractor vs. employee) and suite-rental
> arrangements have significant IRS, Minnesota DOLI, tax, and liability
> consequences; do not rely on this output to classify a worker or draft a lease.
> Verify Minnesota cosmetology/esthetician/eyelash licensing and any required
> agreements with the Minnesota Board of Cosmetologist Examiners and a qualified
> attorney/accountant before hiring, contracting, or leasing.

## Guardrails

- **Same questions, same trial, same scorecard for every candidate** — fairness
  and predictive validity both come from standardization.
- **Score observable behavior**, never "vibes" — use the anchors.
- **Never assert a worker is a 1099 contractor** to save cost — that's a legal
  determination with real penalties if wrong. Flag it for a professional.
- **Require proof of valid MN licensure** for anyone performing lash services.
