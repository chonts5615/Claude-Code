# Worker Classification & Licensing — what to verify

**This is the legally load-bearing part. Nothing here is legal, tax, or HR
advice.** It exists to help you *ask the right questions* and *gather the right
documents* before a professional makes the call. Worker misclassification carries
real IRS and Minnesota penalties; licensing violations risk fines and shutdowns.
Confirm everything below with a qualified attorney/accountant and the Minnesota
Board of Cosmetologist Examiners.

## Independent contractor vs. employee (why it matters)

Calling someone a "1099 contractor" does **not** make them one — the
relationship's substance controls, and getting it wrong means back taxes,
penalties, and liability. The IRS uses **common-law factors** in three buckets;
many states (including Minnesota) apply their own, sometimes stricter, tests.
Walk the facts; don't assume.

| Bucket | Question | Leans EMPLOYEE | Leans CONTRACTOR |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Behavioral control** | Who controls how/when the work is done? | You set hours, methods, dress, train them | They set their own hours/methods |
| **Financial control** | Who controls the economics? | You provide tools/supplies, pay hourly/salary, they can't profit/loss | They invest in their own tools, set prices, bear profit/loss, can work elsewhere |
| **Relationship** | What does the arrangement look like? | Ongoing, exclusive, core to your business, benefits | Project/independent, non-exclusive, written contractor agreement |

A **booth/suite renter** generally leans contractor *only if it's genuinely their
own business*: they pay you rent (you don't pay them), set their own prices and
hours, keep their own client revenue, use their own products, hold their own
license and insurance, and book their own clients. The more you control schedule,
pricing, branding, or supplies, the more it looks like employment regardless of
the label. Minnesota and the IRS both look past the paperwork to these facts.

**Document set (confirm specifics with a pro):**
- *Employee:* W-4, I-9, state new-hire reporting, payroll, workers' comp,
  unemployment insurance.
- *Contractor/renter:* signed W-9, a written independent-contractor **or** suite-
  lease agreement, proof of their own license + liability insurance, their own
  booking/payment setup. You issue a 1099 for contractor payments (not for rent
  you *receive*).

## Minnesota licensing (verify current rules with the Board)

Lash extensions are a **licensed** service in Minnesota. From the Minnesota Board
of Cosmetologist Examiners framework (MN Statutes ch. 155A; MN Rules ch. 2105 —
**re-verify current hours/requirements directly with the Board**):

- A person performing eyelash extensions must hold an appropriate **individual
  license** — **cosmetologist, esthetician, advanced practice esthetician, or
  eyelash technician**.
- A licensed esthetician/advanced practice esthetician or cosmetologist generally
  may perform lash extensions without a separate eyelash-technician license
  (confirm).
- A standalone **eyelash technician** path exists (commonly described as a
  fundamentals course plus a Board-approved course covering eyelash health,
  safety, infection control, and law/regulation) — **hours and exact
  requirements change; verify with the Board before relying on a number.**
- The **business/salon** itself typically needs a **salon license**, and each
  independent suite operating as its own salon may need its own salon license —
  confirm how the Board treats a multi-suite building.
- Sanitation/infection-control rules apply to the space and tools.

Always: copy and record each worker's/renter's license number and expiration,
and confirm the license type actually covers lash services.

## Practical guardrails for Bloom

- **Never classify to cut cost.** If the facts say employee, contractor paperwork
  won't protect you.
- **Renters are renters only if they run their own business** — rent flows to you,
  they control their work and clients, hold their own license + insurance.
- **Get the lease/contract reviewed by a Minnesota attorney** before use; don't
  ship a template as final.
- **Verify licensure every time**, at hire/move-in and at renewal.
- When in doubt, write the question down and route it to the
  attorney/accountant/Board — that's the right move, not a guess.
